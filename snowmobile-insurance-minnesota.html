Riding the Taconite State Trail and Northern Minnesota Trails

If you ride out of Grand Rapids or the Iron Range, you probably know the Taconite State Trail. According to the Minnesota DNR, it stretches 165 miles from Grand Rapids to Ely and meets the David Dill/Arrowhead State Trail just west of Lake Vermilion. After the first six paved miles out of Grand Rapids, the natural-surface trail is used mainly for snowmobiling in winter, winding through state and national forest land and linking to McCarthy Beach, Lake Vermilion-Soudan Underground Mine, and Bear Head Lake state parks.

Before you head out, check your registration. The DNR lets Minnesota owners register a sled for trail use or non-trail use, and a sled with a non-trail use registration may not be operated on state or grant-in-aid trails. A snowmobile that isn’t registered in Minnesota needs a snowmobile state trail pass to ride those trails. See the DNR snowmobile registration page for current rules.

Long trail days mean more miles, more trees, more traffic, and more time hauling a trailer. We’ll help you review liability, physical damage, and trailer coverage so your policy fits how — and where — you ride.

Snowmobile Insurance Questions from Itasca County & Iron Range Riders

Do I need insurance for my snowmobile in Minnesota?

Trail systems and landowners often expect proof of liability. Even when not required everywhere you ride, liability plus physical damage and storage coverage help protect you after an accident, theft, or off-season loss.

Will my auto or homeowners insurance cover my snowmobile?

Usually not fully. Auto policies typically don’t extend to snowmobiles, and most homeowners policies exclude or sharply limit recreational vehicles — especially for trail riding or damage to the sled itself. That’s why many riders around Grand Rapids and the Iron Range carry a separate snowmobile or recreational policy. Ask us to check your current policies and we’ll point out any gaps.

Is my sled covered on the trail and while it’s on the trailer?

A snowmobile policy typically covers your sled on the trail, and many also cover it while it’s being hauled. The trailer itself may need to be listed separately, and liability while towing usually ties back to your auto policy. Details vary by carrier, so ask us to check your policy before you haul out to the Taconite State Trail or over to the Iron Range.

What does liability cover if I hit someone or something on the trail?

Liability coverage on a snowmobile policy typically helps pay for injuries to other people or damage to their property when you’re at fault — like hitting another rider or damaging a landowner’s fence. Damage to your own sled falls under collision or comprehensive instead. Limits and exclusions vary by policy, so ask us to check yours and talk through how much liability makes sense for how you ride.

Is winter storage coverage available?

Yes. Ask about storage or laid-up coverage for the months your sled is off the trails — a common need for northern Minnesota riders.

Can I bundle snowmobile with ATV or auto?

Often yes. Chandler is an independent agency — we can discuss bundling snowmobile with ATV, auto, or cabin coverage when carriers allow it.
